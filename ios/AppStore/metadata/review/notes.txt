Lucium is a web browser for iPhone and iPad. Every web page is shown with WKWebView (WebKit); the app has no other web engine (guideline 2.5.6).

SIGN-IN AND TESTING
The app has no account, sign-in, purchase, subscription or advertising: no demo account is needed. Open the app, tap the address field in the bottom bar, type an address or a search, and the page opens.

WHERE TO FIND EACH FEATURE
- Tabs: the right-hand button of the bottom bar opens the tabs sheet (pinned, folders, today, archive).
- Settings: the gear beside the address field (on iPad in landscape, the sidebar's foot): search engine, privacy (protection level, HTTPS first, Global Privacy Control, tracking parameters), lock, spaces, synchronization, licenses.
- Spaces and profiles: the round buttons of the bottom bar; touch and hold one to create a Space and a profile. Two profiles keep separate cookies. iPad in landscape: a permanent sidebar replaces the bar.
- Private tab: "New private tab" in that menu; nothing is kept once it is closed. Command bar: the address field; touch and hold it to clear one site's data.
- Page actions: the three-dots button: Share, Save as PDF, Downloads (the app's Downloads folder, shown in the Files app (Info.plist: UIFileSharingEnabled, LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace); nothing else is kept in Documents).
- Lock: a Space or the app can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode (LocalAuthentication: the app gets only a yes or a no).

THE CONTENT BLOCKER
The blocker uses WKContentRuleList. Its filter lists (public ad and tracker rules) are converted before the build and are inside the app: nothing is downloaded, and they change only with an update. Authors and licenses: the Licenses screen.

JAVASCRIPT THE APP RUNS IN PAGES (guidelines 2.5.2 and 4.7)
The app adds its own scripts to pages with WKUserScript, to hide cookie-consent banners and to apply its lists' blocking rules and its YouTube rules. All of this code is in the app bundle, and no native function of the device is exposed to a web page (no script message handler).

NETWORK
The app makes no connection of its own, except one that is off by default: the gear > Synchronization pairs it with the person's own Mac, over the local network only (any other address is refused; no Bonjour, server, account or internet). The person turns it on and reads the QR code the Mac shows (camera, NSCameraUsageDescription; a code can be typed instead; local network: NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription). Otherwise: no analytics, crash reporting, account or update check, and no download of lists. It connects only to the pages the person opens, loads what they load, and downloads the files the person asks for (WKDownload).

APP TRANSPORT SECURITY
Info.plist sets NSAllowsArbitraryLoadsInWebContent: some pages a browser opens are http. With "HTTPS first" the app asks for https:// first and opens http:// only if the site refuses or stays silent for 5 seconds, and says so. An invalid certificate never falls back to http without the person's tap. NSAllowsArbitraryLoads is not set.

PRIVACY AND ENCRYPTION
PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares no tracking, no data collected and no tracking domain, and none of the required-reason APIs is called. The privacy label: "Data Not Collected". The encryption is what iOS provides: HTTPS (WebKit) and, for the synchronization with the person's own Mac, TLS 1.3 of Network.framework, plus HMAC-SHA-256 and SHA-256 of CryptoKit to authenticate the pairing; the app implements no cipher and no protocol of its own. ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption is false: only the system's encryption is used.

OTHER POINTS
- No donation or payment button (guideline 3.2.2). The default-browser entitlement is not requested.
- YouTube plays; the app's own rules against its ads block only in part. DRM-protected videos may not play.
- Lucium is free software (GPL-3.0-or-later, with an additional permission for distribution through the App Store); the source is public.
